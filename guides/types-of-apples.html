Types of apples and what they taste like: a sweetness chart

By the Taste10 team · Updated November 2026

The sweetest common apples are Fuji, Gala, Golden Delicious, Red Delicious and Ambrosia; the most tart are Granny Smith and McIntosh. Between them sit sweet apples with a little tang (Honeycrisp, Envy, Jonagold, Cox) and balanced sweet-tart ones (Pink Lady, Cosmic Crisp, SweeTango, Jazz, Braeburn). Texture matters as much as sweetness: some are crunchy and dense, others soft and juicy. Apple sweetness chart: from sweet to tart

There are thousands of apple varieties, but most shops sell the same twenty or so. This chart puts the common ones in order, from the sweetest to the most tart, with the texture next to the taste. The descriptions follow the growers, breeders and fruit collections that describe each variety (see Sources). The last column is the quickest way to choose: start with an apple close to one you already enjoy.

Read the chart as bands, not as an exact ranking. Two apples of the same variety can taste quite different, as the next section explains.

What makes an apple sweet or tart

An apple's taste comes from a balance of sugar and acid, mostly malic acid, the main acid in apples. A Granny Smith has a lot of acid; a Fuji or a Gala has little, so its sugar comes through.

Sugar alone does not decide how sweet an apple tastes. A 2017 study at the Fondazione Edmund Mach in Italy compared what a trained tasting panel perceived with what the lab measured, and found that the usual sugar reading (degrees Brix) predicts sweetness only weakly. Sorbitol, a sugar alcohol in the fruit, and aroma compounds, above all fruity esters, also made apples taste sweeter.

Where and how an apple was grown matters too. The UK's National Fruit Collection notes that Red Delicious tastes much better from climates with enough warmth and light, and that Fuji and Braeburn often fail to ripen fully in Britain. An apple picked early or stored for months can taste flatter than the same variety in season.

Crisp, crunchy or soft: the texture side

Many people choose an apple by its bite as much as by its taste. The crunchiest varieties in the chart are Honeycrisp, SweeTango, Cosmic Crisp, Envy and Opal: dense flesh that breaks with a snap. Gala is crisp but has less crunch. McIntosh is tender and very juicy, which is why it cooks down so quickly.

A soft, dry, floury bite is called mealy. It comes with overripe or long-stored apples rather than with one variety, so the same apple can be crisp in October and mealy months later. More on texture words: mouthfeel.

Best apples for eating raw and for baking

  • For eating raw: Gala, Fuji, Honeycrisp, Ambrosia, Pink Lady, Cosmic Crisp, SweeTango and Jazz. Red Delicious is best eaten fresh or in salads.
  • For pies and tarts: Granny Smith, which works in pies as well as raw. The US Apple Association suggests mixing Golden Delicious with Granny Smith for a pie that is both sweet and sharp.
  • For apple sauce: McIntosh, which falls apart when cooked; Honeycrisp and Granny Smith work too. If you use McIntosh in a pie, cut thick slices.

How to choose apples at the shop

  1. Decide sweet or tart. If you do not like sour fruit, start with the first band of the chart; if you like a bite of acid, start at the bottom.
  2. Decide on the texture. Dense and crunchy (Honeycrisp, SweeTango, Cosmic Crisp) or tender and juicy (McIntosh, Golden Delicious).
  3. Start from what you know. Use the last column of the chart: if you like Gala, Envy and Fuji are close; if you like Granny Smith, try Pink Lady or Braeburn for a little more sweetness.
  4. Buy one before a bag. Try a single apple of a new variety before buying a whole bag of it.
  5. Mind the season. Most varieties are harvested between midsummer and autumn and sold from storage for the rest of the year, so the same variety can taste fresher soon after harvest.

Questions and answers

What is the sweetest apple?

Among common varieties, Fuji, Gala, Golden Delicious, Red Delicious and Ambrosia are the sweetest: they have little acid, so their sugar comes through. Honeycrisp is also sweet, with only a hint of tang.

What is the most tart apple?

Granny Smith is the most tart common apple, followed by McIntosh. Pink Lady, Braeburn, Jazz and Cosmic Crisp are sweet-tart: sharp, but with clear sweetness.

What is the crunchiest apple?

Honeycrisp, SweeTango and Cosmic Crisp are known for their crunch, followed by Envy and Opal. McIntosh and Golden Delicious are softer.

What is the difference between Fuji and Gala?

Both are sweet with little acid. Fuji is firmer, with a mild sweetness; Gala is a little softer, very sweet and more aromatic.

What is the difference between Honeycrisp and Gala?

Honeycrisp is larger, juicier and much crunchier, with a honeyed taste and a hint of tang. Gala is softer, smaller and sweeter, with a more aromatic flavour.

What are the best apples for baking?

Granny Smith is the classic choice, often mixed with a sweet apple such as Golden Delicious. McIntosh falls apart when cooked, which suits apple sauce better than pie.

Why does the same apple variety taste different from bag to bag?

Ripeness at picking, the climate where it grew and how long it was stored all change the balance of sugar, acid and aroma. A Fuji or Braeburn grown somewhere cool may never ripen fully.
