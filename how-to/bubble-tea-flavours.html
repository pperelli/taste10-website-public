Bubble tea flavours explained: how to order boba you'll enjoy

By the Taste10 team · Updated November 2026

Bubble tea (boba) is iced tea, usually with milk or fruit, served with chewy tapioca pearls at the bottom. To order one you will enjoy, choose creamy milk tea or light fruit tea first, then a flavour close to something you already like, then the sweetness and one topping. For a first try, a classic milk tea or brown sugar milk with pearls at 50% sugar is the easiest start. Or photograph the menu board with Taste10, which ranks the drinks for your personal taste.

What is bubble tea?

Bubble tea, also called boba, is a cold tea drink from Taiwan with chewy balls at the bottom, drunk through a wide straw. It appeared in the 1980s: two Taiwanese tea houses, Chun Shui Tang in Taichung and Hanlin Tea Room in Tainan, both claim to have invented it, and a 2019 court case did not credit either of them.

Every bubble tea is built from the same parts:

  • A tea base: black, green or oolong tea.
  • Milk or fruit: milk teas use fresh or powdered milk (some shops offer almond, soy or coconut milk); fruit teas leave the milk out and use fruit, syrup or powder for the flavour.
  • Sugar and ice, which many shops let you choose.
  • A topping: most often tapioca pearls, made from the starch of the cassava root and cooked until soft, chewy and translucent. Most are dark because of the brown sugar added to them.

The pearls themselves taste of little more than that sugar. What people come back for is the chew, and the flavour of the drink around it.

Milk teas are creamy and filling, closer to a dessert. Fruit teas are lighter, sharper and more refreshing. If you are not sure which you are in the mood for, that is the first thing to decide.

Bubble tea toppings, from chewy to bursting

The topping changes a drink as much as the flavour does. If chewy textures are not for you, choose one that pops or melts instead.

ToppingTexture and taste
Tapioca pearls (boba)Chewy and elastic, sweetened with brown sugar. The classic "bubble", made from cassava starch.
Popping bobaA thin skin around fruit juice that bursts when you bite. Flavours include mango, strawberry and lychee.
Grass jellyA dark herbal jelly from a plant of the mint family: mild and slightly bitter.
Coconut jelly (nata de coco)Chewy cubes grown by bacteria in coconut water, then soaked in syrup. A dessert from the Philippines; firmer than pearls, mildly sweet.
Egg puddingSoft custard that sinks to the bottom and makes a milk tea richer.
Red beanSoft, sweet azuki bean paste with an earthy taste.
Aloe veraSoft, juicy cubes with a mild, fresh sweetness.
Cheese foamA salty-sweet cream cap on top, sipped before the tea. Closer to salted whipped cream than to cheese.

Popping boba is made by spherification: drops of juice mixed with sodium alginate meet a calcium solution, which forms a thin, soft skin around each one.

How to order bubble tea in 5 steps

  1. Choose milk tea or fruit tea. Creamy and rich, or light and refreshing.
  2. Pick a flavour. Use the table above: start with one that matches something you already like, not the most unusual name on the board.
  3. Choose the sweetness. Many shops offer sugar levels in steps of 25%: 0, 25, 50, 75 or 100%. Full sugar is very sweet for many first-timers; 50% lets the tea taste through.
  4. Choose the ice. Usually no, less, normal or more ice. Less ice means more drink and less watering down as it melts.
  5. Add one topping. Tapioca pearls for the classic; popping boba or a jelly if you want something different. One topping is plenty the first time.

Which bubble tea should I try first?

For a first bubble tea, a classic black milk tea or a brown sugar milk with tapioca pearls at 50% sugar is the easiest start: familiar tastes, nothing too unusual. If you prefer something light, try mango or passion fruit green tea. Taro, matcha and cheese foam are the flavours people tend to love or not like at all, so taste a sip from a friend's cup before ordering a whole one.

Three things that often spoil a first try: full sugar when you do not usually like sweet drinks, three toppings at once, and choosing a flavour only because its name sounds interesting.

Questions and answers

What is bubble tea made of?

Tea (black, green or oolong), usually milk or a fruit flavour, sugar, ice, and chewy tapioca pearls made from the starch of the cassava root.

What does bubble tea taste like?

A classic milk tea tastes like sweet iced tea with milk. The pearls taste mostly of brown sugar; their appeal is the chewy texture. Fruit teas taste like sweet iced fruit tea.

What is the best bubble tea flavour for beginners?

A classic black milk tea or a brown sugar milk with tapioca pearls at 50% sugar. For something lighter, mango or passion fruit green tea.

What is taro bubble tea?

A milk tea flavoured with taro, a starchy root vegetable. It is pale purple, sweet, creamy and slightly nutty, more like a dessert than a tea.

What is popping boba?

Small balls with a thin skin filled with fruit juice, which burst when you bite them. They are made by spherification, with sodium alginate and calcium.
