Coffee drinks explained: how to pick one you'll enjoy

By the Taste10 team · Updated November 2026

Most coffee-shop drinks are espresso with different amounts of milk, foam, water or sweetness. The more milk and sweetness, the milder the coffee taste: a mocha or latte is the gentlest, a cappuccino and flat white taste more of coffee, and an espresso is the strongest. To pick one you will enjoy, start from how you already take your coffee, then choose the size, the milk and the sweetness. Or photograph the menu board with Taste10, which ranks the drinks for your personal taste.

What are the main types of coffee drinks?

Almost every drink on a coffee-shop menu is built from the same few parts, and the names mostly tell you how much of each is in the cup:

  • A coffee base: usually espresso, a small, concentrated shot made under pressure. Some drinks use filter coffee or cold brew instead.
  • Milk: none, a spoonful, or a lot. Steamed milk with fine bubbles (microfoam) feels velvety; a thick cap of foam feels light and airy.
  • Water or ice, which make a drink longer or colder.
  • Extras: chocolate, caramel or flavoured syrups, which make a drink sweeter and the coffee taste milder.

The more milk and sweetness a drink has, the milder its coffee taste. That one rule explains most of the menu.

Latte, cappuccino or flat white: what is the difference?

All three are espresso with steamed milk. What changes is how much milk there is and how it is foamed:

Flat whiteCappuccinoLatte
MilkLess milk than a latte, silkyFrothed milk under the foamThe most milk
FoamA thin layer of microfoamA thick capA thin layer of fine foam
SizeSmaller than a latteAbout 150 ml in ItalyThe largest of the three
Coffee tasteStrongest of the threeClear, under an airy topMildest of the three

Chain shops often serve much bigger cups. A bigger cup usually means more milk, not more coffee, so the drink tastes milder.

Macchiato or latte? An espresso macchiato is a small espresso with a spot of milk, almost pure coffee. A latte macchiato is the opposite: a tall glass of milk with espresso poured in. Many chains also sell a caramel macchiato, a sweet, milky drink with caramel. If you want a strong coffee, ask for an espresso macchiato; if you want something mild and milky, a latte or latte macchiato.

Cortado or flat white? Both have a lot of coffee for their size. A cortado is smaller, coffee with just a little warm milk; a flat white has more milk and a thin, silky top.

From mild to strong: where to start

Roughly ordered by how much coffee you taste, from the mildest to the strongest:

  1. Mocha and caramel macchiato: sweet and milky; the chocolate or caramel leads.
  2. Latte and latte macchiato: mostly milk, a soft coffee taste.
  3. Cappuccino: milky and airy, with a clearer coffee taste.
  4. Flat white: milk, but the espresso leads.
  5. Cortado: mostly coffee, softened with a little milk.
  6. Espresso macchiato: espresso with a spot of milk.
  7. Americano and cold brew: black coffee, without milk to soften it.
  8. Espresso and ristretto: the most concentrated coffee taste.

If you usually take your coffee with milk and sugar, start at the top. Step one place down when a drink starts to taste too much like warm milk. Black coffee is a classic acquired taste: many people only learn to like it over time.

How to order at a coffee shop in 5 steps

  1. Choose hot or cold. Most espresso drinks can be made iced; cold brew and iced coffee are cold from the start.
  2. Choose how milky. No milk (espresso, americano), a little (macchiato, cortado, flat white) or a lot (cappuccino, latte). Use the ladder above.
  3. Choose the size and the shots. In a bigger cup the extra space is mostly milk. If a large drink tastes too mild, ask for an extra shot of espresso.
  4. Choose the milk. Many shops offer plant-based milks such as oat, soy or almond as well as cow's milk; each changes the taste a little.
  5. Decide on sweetness. Syrups (vanilla, caramel, hazelnut) and chocolate sweeten a drink and soften the coffee. Ask for less syrup if you are not sure.

One thing that surprises travellers: in Italian, "latte" simply means milk (caffè latte is short for "coffee and milk"). In Italy, ask for a caffè latte.

Which coffee should I try first?

If you are new to coffee, a latte or a mocha is the easiest start: milky, smooth, and in the mocha's case sweet. If you already drink milky coffee and want more flavour, try a flat white or a cortado next. If you like your coffee black, compare an americano and an espresso to see whether you prefer it long and light or short and intense.

Three things that often spoil a first try: ordering an espresso macchiato when you expected a big milky drink, a large cup of a strong drink you have never tasted, and several syrups at once.

Questions and answers

What is the difference between a latte and a cappuccino?

Both are espresso with steamed milk. A cappuccino is smaller with a thick cap of foam, so it tastes airier and more of coffee; a latte has more milk and only a thin layer of foam, so it tastes creamier and milder.

What is the difference between a flat white and a latte?

A flat white is smaller, with less milk and a thin layer of silky microfoam, so the espresso tastes stronger. A latte is bigger and milkier, with a milder coffee taste.

What is a macchiato?

An espresso macchiato is an espresso with a spot of foamed milk on top; the name means "with a spot of milk". A latte macchiato is the opposite: a glass of steamed milk with espresso poured in. A caramel macchiato is a sweet, milky chain-shop drink with caramel.

What is a cortado?

A Spanish coffee: espresso "cut" with a little warm milk, served small. Spain’s dictionary defines café cortado as coffee with very little milk, so the coffee taste stays strong.

Which coffee drink is the least bitter?

Milky and sweet drinks such as a mocha, latte or caramel macchiato, where milk and chocolate or caramel soften the bitterness. Among the black coffees, a ristretto is described as less bitter than a full espresso, though more intense.

What is the strongest coffee drink?

In taste, an espresso or ristretto, because they are the most concentrated. In caffeine per cup, it depends on the size: a 30 ml espresso has about 65 mg, a 240 ml cup of filter coffee about 95 mg, and a red eye combines both.
