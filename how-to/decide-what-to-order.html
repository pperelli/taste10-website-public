How to decide what to order at a restaurant

By the Taste10 team · Updated November 2026

Narrow the menu before you choose: decide what kind of meal you want, rule out what does not fit, keep three dishes, then pick the one that suits your taste best. Choosing is hard because a long menu means too many options to compare; a short list and a clear reason make it easy.

Why choosing from a menu is so hard

Psychologists call it choice overload. In a well-known experiment, Sheena Iyengar and Mark Lepper set up a tasting stand in a supermarket: shoppers who saw 24 jams were far less likely to buy one than shoppers who saw 6. Later research found the effect is far from universal: across many studies it is small on average, and it shows up mainly when the options are hard to compare and people have no clear preference. That describes a long menu, or a menu from a cuisine you do not know, almost exactly.

7 ways to decide faster

  1. Decide the kind of meal first: light or filling, meat, fish or vegetables, something familiar or something new.
  2. Rule out instead of picking in. Crossing off whole sections is quicker than comparing forty dishes.
  3. Keep three. Three candidates can be compared; ten cannot.
  4. Ask what the kitchen is known for, or what the server would order.
  5. Look at what other tables are eating.
  6. Share. Two dishes for two people turns one decision into a tasting.
  7. Check your three against your own taste: which one fits what you like best?

When nothing on the menu appeals

The opposite problem has the same cure. Look for a familiar base, such as rice, noodles, bread or grilled meat, ask whether a dish can come with the sauce on the side, and start with a small plate. A guide to eating well as a picky eater has more ideas.

Questions and answers

Why is it so hard to decide what to eat?

Long menus with many similar options make it hard to compare, especially when you are not sure what you feel like. Narrowing the choice to three dishes helps most people decide quickly.

Is the most popular dish the best choice?

It is the dish most people liked, not necessarily the one you will like most. Taste differs from person to person, so it is worth checking against your own taste.

What should I order at a restaurant I have never been to?

Ask what the kitchen is known for, look at other tables, and choose between the dishes that sound closest to what you enjoy.
