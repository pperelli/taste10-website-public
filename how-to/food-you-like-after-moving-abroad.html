How to find food you like after moving abroad

By the Taste10 team · Updated November 2026

Do not try to replace everything from home at once. Pick the few foods you miss most and look for their closest local versions, learn the local staples one at a time, and check unfamiliar products before you buy them, so your weekly shop stops being a gamble.

Why food is one of the hardest parts of moving

In a 2025 study of international students in Norway, missing the taste of home was one of five main themes, next to food prices, language barriers and simply not knowing what a product is. The study's title quotes one of them: "I bought it, but I still don't know what it is." People who move for work describe the same thing: the weekly shop takes twice as long, and part of it ends up in the bin.

7 steps to eating well in a new country

  1. List what you miss, and why. Not "bread from home" but what about it: the dense crumb, the sour taste, the crust.
  2. Find the local cousin. Most foods have a close relative in another country (see the table below).
  3. Learn one local staple a week: the bread, the cheese, the breakfast. Try two or three versions side by side.
  4. Ask locals what they buy: colleagues and neighbours know the brands worth trying.
  5. Keep the international shops for the foods nothing replaces. They cost more, so use them for comfort, not for everything.
  6. Keep a list of hits and misses, with a photo of each pack you liked. Six months later it is your own local shopping list.
  7. Check before you buy, especially when you cannot read the label.

Questions and answers

How long does it take to get used to food in a new country?

It differs from person to person. Learning one local staple at a time, and keeping a few comforts from home, makes the change easier.

Where can I find food from home abroad?

In the international aisle of large supermarkets, in shops for a particular cuisine, and from online shops that deliver food from your home country.

Can Taste10 read labels in the local language?
