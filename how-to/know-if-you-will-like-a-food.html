How to know if you'll like a food before you buy it

By the Taste10 team · Updated November 2026

Compare it with foods you already know you like: its main taste (sweet, sour, bitter, salty, savoury or spicy), its texture and its smell. Star ratings tell you whether other people liked it, not whether you will, so a prediction based on your own taste is the most useful clue you can get.

Why reviews and star ratings often get it wrong for you

A 4.8-star rating is an average of strangers' palates. Taste differs from person to person in ways nobody chooses: sensitivity to some bitter tastes is partly genetic, and for some people coriander tastes of soap because of a gene near their smell receptors. So a food that most people love can honestly be a 3 for you, and one with mixed reviews can be your new favourite (more in why the same food can be a 9 for you and a 3 for someone else).

5 steps before you buy

  1. Name the closest food you know. "Like a mango but more sour" is already a prediction.
  2. Check the main taste against what you like: do you enjoy sour, bitter or very sweet?
  3. Check the texture. For many people texture decides more than taste.
  4. Smell it if you can, at a fruit stall or a cheese counter. Much of what we call taste is really smell.
  5. Buy the smallest size, or get a prediction for your own taste first.

Questions and answers

Can you tell from the smell whether you will like a food?

Often, yes. Much of what we call taste is really smell, so a food whose smell you enjoy has a good chance.

Why do I dislike a food that everyone else loves?

Taste differs from person to person, partly for genetic reasons. A food can be excellent and still not suit your palate.

Are star ratings useful for food?

They tell you whether many people liked something. They cannot tell you whether you will, because they average many different tastes.
