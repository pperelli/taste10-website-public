How to eat well abroad as a picky eater

By the Taste10 team · Updated November 2026

Prepare before you go: learn the few dishes of that cuisine that are closest to what you eat at home. On the spot, look for simple preparations such as grilled, fried, rice, noodles and bread, ask for sauces on the side, and check a dish against your taste before you order, not after.

Why travel is hard for picky eaters

At home you know your safe places. Abroad, menus are in another language, dishes look different from what their names suggest, and every meal is a gamble you pay for. Many picky eaters end up eating the same thing every day, or living on snacks, and miss half of what the trip could be.

Before you go

  1. Learn five dishes of the cuisine that sound close to what you like. Our Eating Abroad guides explain the classics of several cuisines.
  2. Learn the base of the cuisine: rice, noodles, flatbread, grilled meat. Almost every cuisine has a plain version of its base that you can build on.
  3. Learn two phrases: "sauce on the side" and "not spicy". They solve more problems than a whole phrasebook.
  4. Pack a familiar snack for long travel days, so you never have to choose a meal out of hunger.

At the restaurant or market

  • Choose simple preparations. Grilled, fried or steamed tells you more than an unfamiliar sauce.
  • Look at other tables. Seeing a dish is often easier than reading about it.
  • Start small. Street food in small portions, skewers or dumplings, is a cheap way to try something new.
  • Read the description, not only the name. Words such as fermented, pickled or sour tell you a lot before the plate arrives.
  • Check before you order, with a prediction for your own taste.

Recipes differ from one place to the next: a dish that is mild in one restaurant can be spicy in another, so read the description or ask.

Questions and answers

What should a picky eater eat abroad?

Start with the simplest version of the local base, such as rice, noodles, flatbread or grilled meat, and dishes close to what you eat at home. Then add one new thing at a time.

Is street food a good idea for picky eaters?

Often, yes. Portions are small and cheap, and you can see the food before you buy it, so a dish you do not like costs little.
