How to stop being a picky eater as an adult

By the Taste10 team · Updated November 2026

Start from what you already love and move one small step at a time. Choose new foods that share something with your favourites, such as the same texture or the same main flavour, and taste them in small amounts more than once: liking often grows with every try.

Why some adults eat a narrow range of foods

Reluctance to try new foods has a name: food neophobia. The psychologists Patricia Pliner and Karen Hobden built a scale to measure it in 1992 and found it was linked to anxiety and to how much experience someone already had with unusual foods. In other words, it is common, it is partly about uncertainty, and it can change with experience.

Taste also differs between people for reasons nobody chooses. Some people find coriander soapy because of a gene near their smell receptors, and sensitivity to some bitter tastes runs in families. A food you dislike may simply clash with your palate, which is why the goal is to find your next foods, not to force down everyone else's (see why the same food can be a 9 for you and a 3 for someone else).

8 steps to stop being a picky eater

  1. Start from your favourites. Write down five foods you love and what they have in common: crunchy, creamy, sweet, salty, mild, smoky.
  2. Take the next small step, not a leap. If you love roast chicken, try chicken satay before sea urchin. A new food that shares a texture or a flavour with something you like has a much better chance.
  3. Taste small amounts, more than once. In a review of 43 studies, Appleton and colleagues found that tasting a vegetable repeatedly made people like it more. One bite on one day is not a verdict.
  4. Change one thing at a time. A familiar dish with one new ingredient, or a new ingredient cooked a way you already like: roasted, fried or in a sauce you know.
  5. Check whether it is the texture. For many people the problem is not the taste but the feel: slimy, chewy, mushy. Try the same food prepared differently, such as mushrooms fried until crisp instead of stewed.
  6. Try new food when you are relaxed and hungry, not under pressure at a dinner party.
  7. Keep a record of what you tried and how it was. It shows progress and patterns you did not expect, such as liking sour more than you thought.
  8. Know before you order. For many people the biggest barrier is paying for a whole dish they end up not eating. A personal prediction takes most of that risk away.

Questions and answers

Can you stop being a picky eater as an adult?

Many adults widen the range of foods they enjoy with small steps and repeated tasting. How far you go is up to you: the aim is to enjoy more, not to eat everything.

How many times do I need to try a new food?

There is no fixed number. Studies on repeated tasting show that liking often grows over several tries, so do not judge a food on the first bite. If it still clashes after a few tries, it may simply not suit your palate.

What if picky eating is a real problem in my daily life?

This guide is about taste and curiosity. If eating is a strain in your daily life, talk to a doctor.

Can an app tell me which foods I will like?

Taste10 predicts how much you personally are likely to enjoy a food, drink or dish, with the reason why. It is a prediction, and your own ratings make it fit you better over time.
