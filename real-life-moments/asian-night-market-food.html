I'm at a night market with 30 stalls and room for three dishes

By the Taste10 team · Updated November 2026

Walk the whole market once before you buy anything, note the stalls with long local queues, and share small portions so you can try more. Start with something close to what you know, such as grilled skewers, dumplings or fried snacks, add one new thing, and keep sweets and drinks for the end. A photo of a stall’s menu board in Taste10 ranks its dishes for your personal taste before you queue.

The moment

Steam, smoke and noise in every direction. Dozens of stalls, most of them with handwritten signs, many with queues, all of them smelling good. You are hungry, you have room for maybe three dishes, and every stall you pass makes the last one look like the wrong choice. Asian night markets are some of the best places to eat while travelling, and some of the hardest to choose in.

What usually goes wrong

  • Filling up at the first stall, then walking past five better ones with no room left.
  • Ordering a full portion of something new and discovering after two bites that it is not for you.
  • Queuing for twenty minutes at the most famous stall for a dish you do not actually enjoy.
  • Heat you did not expect: chilli is often added at the stall, generously.

How to choose your stalls

  1. Walk the whole market once, without buying. Most markets are a loop or a long street.
  2. Watch where locals queue. A long local queue is the best review a stall can get.
  3. Plan three dishes: one you know you like, one new thing, one sweet or drink.
  4. Share and go small. Many stalls sell small portions or single skewers; split them with your group.
  5. Ask for less chilli when you order, or point to the sauce and shake your head. You can always add more.

Questions and answers

What food is at an Asian night market?

Usually grilled skewers, dumplings and steamed buns, fried snacks, noodles and fried rice, savoury pancakes, some strong-tasting specialities, and sweets and drinks such as shaved ice and bubble tea.

How do you choose what to eat at a night market?

Walk the whole market first, follow the local queues, plan one familiar dish, one new one and one sweet, and share small portions so you can try more.

What is a hawker centre?

A large food centre in Singapore with many small stalls, where people from different backgrounds eat side by side. UNESCO listed Singapore’s hawker culture as intangible cultural heritage in 2020.

Is night market food spicy?

Some is, much is not. Chilli is often added at the stall, so ask for less or none when you order.
