I'm at a Christmas market abroad and don't know what any of the stalls sell

By the Taste10 team · Updated November 2026

Walk one loop before you buy anything, eat something savoury first, such as a bratwurst in a bun or potato pancakes, then share one sweet, such as roasted almonds or gingerbread, and pick one hot drink. Drinks come in a mug with a deposit you get back when you return it. To know which stalls suit you before you queue, photograph a stall’s board with Taste10: it ranks what the stall sells for your personal taste.

The moment

It is dark by five, your fingers are cold, and the square is packed. Wooden stalls with fairy lights in every direction, the smell of grilled sausages, cinnamon and hot wine all at once, and signs in a language you only half read. Your friends want to eat now, the queues are long, and you have one evening here. Every stall looks good, and you have no idea which ones you will actually enjoy.

What usually goes wrong

  • Buying the first sweet thing you see on an empty stomach, then having no appetite left for the savoury food.
  • Queuing twenty minutes in the cold for a famous stall, for a dish that turns out not to be your kind of thing.
  • Ordering a full portion of something unfamiliar, such as a whole bag of gingerbread or a plate of melted cheese, after one bite of which you know.
  • Forgetting the mug deposit: hot drinks usually come in a mug or cup you pay a deposit on, and many people carry it home without meaning to.

How to choose what to eat and drink

  1. Walk one loop first, without buying. Most markets have a savoury part and a sweet part, and the best stalls are not always at the entrance.
  2. Eat something savoury first: a sausage in a bun, potato pancakes or a slice of flatbread. It warms you up and keeps the sweets for later.
  3. Share the sweets. Roasted almonds, gingerbread and stollen come in bags or slices, and a few bites each is enough to know.
  4. Pick one hot drink, and know there is usually an alcohol-free punch beside the mulled wine.
  5. Keep the mug until you leave, then hand it back at any drinks stall for your deposit, or keep it as a souvenir.

Each region has its own specialities. Nuremberg has grilled its small sausages over beech wood for centuries, and since 2003 they are a protected regional product in the European Union, as is the Dresdner Christstollen, a raisin stollen baked only in and around Dresden. Christmas markets reach well beyond Germany: Strasbourg in France has held one since 1570, with its own Alsatian biscuits and mulled wine.

Mugs, deposits and paying

Hot drinks usually come in a mug or reusable cup with a deposit. In Strasbourg it is one euro, refunded at any stand that sells drinks. Strasbourg says nearly all its stalls take cards, but the cash machines nearby are busy, so bring some cash anyway; smaller markets elsewhere may take cash only.

Questions and answers

What food is at a German Christmas market?

Mostly grilled bratwurst in a bun, potato pancakes, flammkuchen, melted cheese dishes, and sweets such as gingerbread (Lebkuchen), stollen, roasted almonds, roasted chestnuts and chimney cake, with mulled wine and alcohol-free punch to drink.

What should I eat first at a Christmas market?

Something savoury, such as a bratwurst or potato pancakes. It warms you up, and the sweets taste better after it than on an empty stomach.

What does Glühwein taste like?

Sweet and spiced: hot red or white wine with cinnamon, cloves and orange. The alcohol-free version, Kinderpunsch, is made with fruit juice.

What is Lebkuchen?

German gingerbread: a soft, spiced honey cake with cinnamon, cloves and nuts. Nuremberg has baked it for more than 600 years.

What does "drei im Weggla" mean?

It is Franconian for "three in a bun", the way Nuremberg serves its small bratwurst sausages.

Do you pay a deposit on the mugs?

Usually, yes. You pay a deposit with your drink and get it back when you return the mug to a drinks stall. In Strasbourg it is one euro.
