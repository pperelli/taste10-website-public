The moment

A round table, eight people and one menu going around. The rule is simple: everyone picks one dish, and everything is shared. Your turn is coming. You want to pick something you will enjoy, but also something the table will be glad you ordered, and not the fourth fried dish in a row.

How family-style dining works

Eating family style means that dishes come to the middle of the table and everyone takes a little of each, often with rice or bread for everyone. It is how meals are usually eaten in Chinese, Korean, Indian, Middle Eastern and Spanish restaurants (where small plates are called tapas), and it is common at group dinners everywhere. A common rule of thumb: about one dish per person, plus rice or bread.

What usually goes wrong

  • Everyone orders the same kind of dish: three fried ones, two curries, no vegetables.
  • You order something only you will eat, or something you do not enjoy, just to seem easy-going.
  • The heat is wrong for half the table.
  • The dish you were counting on runs out before it reaches you.

What to do in the next five minutes

  1. Listen to the orders before yours. Note what the table already has.
  2. Fill the gap using the table below.
  3. Choose something you would happily eat a lot of. Your pick is the dish you can count on if the rest is not for you.
  4. Ask about heat if the table includes people who do not eat chilli.
  5. Make sure there is rice, noodles or bread. It ties the meal together.

Dim sum: the classic shared meal

Dim sum, probably Cantonese in origin, are small steamed and fried dishes eaten with tea, usually ordered a few at a time and shared. In many restaurants you tick dishes on an order sheet or choose from passing trolleys. Order a few, see what the table likes, then order more of it.

Questions and answers

What is family style dining?

A way of eating where dishes come to the middle of the table and everyone shares them, usually with rice or bread. It is the normal way to eat in many Chinese, Korean, Indian and Middle Eastern restaurants.

How many dishes should you order for a group?

A common rule of thumb is about one dish per person, plus rice, noodles or bread. Order a few more later if the table is still hungry.

How do you order dim sum?

Order a few dishes at a time, from a menu sheet or the passing trolleys, share them with tea, and order more of the ones the table likes.

What should I order when sharing plates?

A dish that fills a gap on the table, such as vegetables, noodles or something fresh, and that you would happily eat a lot of yourself.
