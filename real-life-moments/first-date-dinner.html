I'm on a first date and can't decide what to order

By the Taste10 team · Updated November 2026

Order something you already know you like, that is easy to eat while you talk, and that is not far above your date’s choice in price. Leave very messy, very hot or completely unfamiliar dishes for another evening, and decide early so the menu does not take over the conversation. If two dishes tempt you, a photo of the menu in Taste10 ranks them for your personal taste in seconds.

The moment

The waiter is coming back for the second time. Your date has already decided. You are still reading the same page, half listening, trying to guess which dish will be good, which one you can eat without a mess, and whether the special is a good idea. The longer it takes, the more the menu becomes the topic of the evening.

A first date is not the night to discover that you dislike coriander or that "medium" heat means something else in this kitchen. You want a meal you enjoy, so you can relax and pay attention to the person opposite you.

What usually goes wrong

  • A dish that is hard to eat while talking: long spaghetti, ribs, whole crab, a tall burger, anything that needs your hands or a bib.
  • A gamble on something completely new: if you do not enjoy it, you either push it around the plate or eat it with a face that says so.
  • Too much heat: a dish hotter than you are used to leaves you with watering eyes and a red face at the worst moment.
  • Strong smells or flavours that stay: raw garlic and raw onion follow you through the evening.
  • Taking too long: the indecision itself is what your date remembers.

How to choose your dish

  1. Skip to the mains first. Pick the main, then decide whether a starter is needed.
  2. Keep two candidates you know you like. A dish you have enjoyed before, or one made of ingredients you always enjoy.
  3. Check how it is eaten. Fork-friendly beats finger food tonight: risotto, a fish fillet, a curry with rice, a salad with cut pieces.
  4. Ask one question, such as "Is this one very spicy?" or "What do you recommend?". It sounds relaxed, not unsure.
  5. Decide before the waiter returns, and put the menu down. Closed menus signal you are ready.

Easy first-date dishes, and the ones to keep for later

Sharing a starter can be a nice way to start a conversation, as long as you both like it. If your date suggests sharing, it is fine to steer towards something you know you enjoy.

Questions and answers

What should you eat on a first date?

Something you already know you like and that is easy to eat while talking: risotto, short pasta, a fish fillet, a mild curry with rice or a salad with cut pieces.

What foods should you avoid on a first date?

Messy dishes such as long spaghetti, ribs or whole crab, food much hotter than you are used to, raw garlic or onion, and completely unfamiliar dishes you may not enjoy.

Is dinner a good first date?

Dinner gives you time to talk, but it also takes longer than a coffee or a drink. If you are nervous about the menu, choosing the dish quickly makes dinner much easier.

Should I order the same as my date?

Only if you would choose it anyway. Ordering what you enjoy makes for a more relaxed evening than copying a dish you are unsure about.
