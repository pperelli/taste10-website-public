I'm at a hotel breakfast buffet abroad with one plate and 40 dishes

By the Taste10 team · Updated November 2026

Walk the whole buffet once before you take a plate, then make your first plate a tasting plate: a small spoonful of four or five dishes you do not know and one you like. Go back for more of the winners. The local breakfast, such as miso soup in Japan, menemen in Turkey or congee in China, is the part worth exploring. To know which dishes suit you before you take them, photograph a counter with Taste10: it ranks the dishes for your personal taste.

The moment

It is your first morning abroad, the breakfast room closes at ten, and you have to leave by half past. In front of you: two long counters, forty dishes, a man frying eggs to order, labels in a language you do not read, and one plate in your hand. Half of it looks like dinner to you. The other guests seem to know exactly what they are doing, and you do not want to stand in the way while you work it out.

What usually goes wrong

  • Filling one plate on the first counter with toast and eggs, then finding the local dishes at the far end when your plate is already full.
  • Taking a big spoonful of something unknown and leaving most of it, which feels wasteful and gets looks.
  • Eating the same familiar breakfast every day of a week abroad, and never finding out what you would have loved.
  • Missing the cooked-to-order station, where the eggs, noodles or pancakes are made fresh, because it has a queue and no sign.

How to choose at a hotel breakfast buffet

  1. Walk the whole buffet once before you pick up a plate. Get a coffee or tea first if you like, and look at every counter, including the hot station.
  2. Make your first plate a tasting plate: one small spoonful of four or five things you do not know, plus one thing you know you like.
  3. Go back for more of the winners. A second small plate is normal at a buffet; a full plate you leave is not.
  4. Read the small labels and ask the staff. Many buffets label each dish, and the person at the egg station can tell you what the hot dishes are.
  5. Remember what you liked. Most hotel buffets serve nearly the same dishes every morning, so day two is where your choices pay off.

A buffet abroad usually mixes three things: an international part (bread, pastries, cereals, yoghurt, fruit, eggs), the local breakfast, and often a few dishes for guests from the countries the hotel sees most. The local part is the one worth exploring: it is the breakfast people in that country actually eat.

Continental, full English, Japanese or Turkish breakfast?

  • Continental breakfast: a light breakfast. Merriam-Webster defines it as rolls or toast and coffee. In hotels it usually means bread, pastries, jam, butter, often cheese and cold cuts, and no cooked dishes.
  • Full English breakfast: cooked and filling: eggs, bacon, sausages, baked beans, grilled tomato, mushrooms, toast, and often black pudding.
  • Japanese breakfast: rice and miso soup with small side dishes, such as grilled fish, pickles, rolled omelette and natto.
  • Turkish breakfast (kahvaltı): many small plates shared at the table: cheeses, olives, tomatoes and cucumbers, jams, honey with clotted cream, eggs such as menemen, sucuk sausage, simit bread and pastries, with tea. The word kahvaltı means "before coffee".

Questions and answers

What is a continental breakfast?

A light breakfast without cooked dishes: bread, rolls or pastries with butter and jam, coffee or tea, and in many hotels also cheese, cold cuts, yoghurt and fruit.

What is in a full English breakfast?

Eggs, bacon, sausages, baked beans, grilled tomato, mushrooms and toast, often with black pudding. It is cooked and filling.

What is a Japanese hotel breakfast?

Usually rice and miso soup with small side dishes such as grilled fish, pickles, a rolled omelette and natto, next to an international part with bread and eggs.

What is a Turkish breakfast?

Many small plates shared at the table: cheeses, olives, tomatoes and cucumbers, jams, honey with clotted cream (bal-kaymak), eggs such as menemen, sucuk sausage, simit bread and pastries, with tea.

How do you eat at a breakfast buffet without wasting food?

Walk the whole buffet first, take small spoonfuls of new dishes on a first tasting plate, and go back for more of what you liked. A second small plate is normal; a full plate left behind is not.

Is it rude to go back to a buffet several times?

No. Going back for a second or third small plate is what a buffet is for, and it wastes less food than one overloaded plate.
