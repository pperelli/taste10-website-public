My partner wants to try a cuisine I’m unsure about

By the Taste10 team · Updated November 2026

Say yes, and plan your own plate: look at the menu online beforehand, pick two dishes close to food you already like, and let your partner order the adventurous ones to share, so you can taste without committing to a whole plate. Ask for sauces and heat on the side. Taste10 can rank the menu for your personal taste before you even leave home.

The moment

Your partner has been talking about this restaurant for weeks: a cuisine they love and you barely know. You do not want to be the one who says no, and you do not want to sit through a dinner of dishes you do not enjoy either. Maybe you tried something from that cuisine once and it was too hot, too sour or too strange. Now the table is booked.

What usually goes wrong

  • Going in without a plan and letting the most adventurous dish on the menu decide for you.
  • Ordering a whole plate of something new, then eating the side dishes and pretending you are full.
  • One bad experience deciding everything: a single dish that was too hot or too strong does not tell you much about a whole cuisine.
  • Turning the evening into a test instead of a dinner.

What to do before and at the table

  1. Look at the menu online beforehand. Five quiet minutes at home beat five rushed minutes with a waiter.
  2. Find your two anchors: dishes close to food you already like (see the table below).
  3. Share the adventurous dishes. Let your partner order what they love, and taste a spoonful.
  4. Ask for sauces, chilli or strong garnishes on the side. You decide how much.
  5. Name one thing you liked at the end, even if it was only the bread. It makes the next visit easier for both of you.

Questions and answers

How do I try new food if I don’t like many things?

Start from what you already love and choose new dishes that share something with it, such as the same texture or main flavour, in small amounts and more than once.

What should I order at a restaurant whose cuisine I don’t know?

Look for dishes close to food you already like, ask the staff for their mildest popular dish, and share the more unusual ones so you only taste a spoonful.

What if I don’t like the food?

Order a side you know, such as rice or bread, eat what you enjoy, and do not judge the whole cuisine by one dish.
